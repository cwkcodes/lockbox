Revenue Stack Explorer

A renewable asset almost never earns "the power price". It earns a stack of separate revenue lines, from separate counterparties, with separate risk. Change the assumptions below and watch what happens — first to revenue, then to how much a bank will lend.

2 · What a bank will lend against it

This is the part that surprises engineers. Revenue is not what determines value — bankable revenue is.

DSCR = Debt Service Cover Ratio = cash available ÷ debt repayments. A lender sets a minimum. Volatile revenue means a higher minimum, which means less debt.

The haircut is the mechanism people miss. A lender does not size debt on expected revenue — it sizes on a downside case. Contracted income is taken at face value; merchant income is cut before it counts, because nobody will underwrite a price and volume nobody controls. Set it to 0% and the distinction between contract structures largely vanishes, which is exactly why it exists.

Maximum debt = (CFADS ÷ target DSCR) × annuity factor, where annuity factor = (1 − (1+r)⁻ⁿ) ÷ r. This is a simplified sculpting calculation: it assumes level cash flow and ignores interest during construction, fees, reserve accounts and tax. A real project finance model handles all four. CFADS here is revenue less operating costs only.

The counter-intuitive bit — sit with this one

Switch between BESS · merchant and BESS · tolled at the default settings. The tolled battery earns about £260,000 a year less. It supports roughly £4.6m more debt — £7.9m against £3.3m, more than double.

Two things cause it, and both are visible in the controls. The toll is contracted, so it escapes the lender's haircut entirely, while merchant revenue is cut by 30% before it counts. And a contracted asset is allowed a lower DSCR — 1.35× rather than 1.60×. The two effects compound.

More debt means a smaller equity cheque for the same physical asset. Since return is measured against equity invested, the lower-revenue structure can produce the higher equity return. That is the moment engineering intuition gives way to financial intuition, and it is the core of infrastructure investing. Nothing about the battery changed — only who carries the risk.

3 · Every term used above

No acronym goes unexplained. The full list of roughly 350 terms is in the repository glossary.

RO / ROC
Renewables Obligation / Renewables Obligation Certificate. The legacy GB subsidy. Suppliers must present certificates to prove they sourced renewable power, so generators can sell them. Closed to new capacity on 31 March 2017, but existing assets receive support for 20 years from accreditation — so you will meet ROC-backed assets in due diligence throughout the 2030s.
Buy-out price
The penalty a supplier pays instead of presenting a ROC. It sets the ceiling on what a ROC is worth. £69.34 for the year to 31 March 2027. The uprating switched from RPI to CPI inflation on 1 April 2026, which permanently reduces the long-run value of every ROC-backed asset in Great Britain.
CfD
Contract for Difference. The current scheme. If the market price is below the agreed strike price the generator is topped up; if it is above, the generator pays money back. That two-way flow is what makes it a contract for difference rather than a subsidy — the generator has sold its price risk.
Strike price
The £/MWh a CfD guarantees. Always ask which year's prices it is quoted in — AR7 used 2024 prices, AR6 used 2012. Comparing the two without adjusting is a common and serious error.
Capture price
The volume-weighted average price an asset actually achieved: Σ(price × volume) ÷ Σ(volume). Not the simple average. Wind and solar earn below the average because they generate when everything like them generates, pushing the price down.
Cannibalisation
That self-inflicted price depression. It deepens as more of the same technology connects. Solar's measured 81.9% capture rate means applying a flat baseload price to solar volume overstates revenue by about 18% before any other adjustment.
REGO
Renewable Energy Guarantees of Origin. One certificate per MWh proving the power was renewable, sold separately from the electricity itself. Worth £1–2/MWh in summer 2026, having peaked at £20–25/MWh in 2023. Lenders give it little or no credit precisely because of that volatility.
Capacity Market
Pays generators and storage to be available at times of system stress, not to generate. Paid on derated capacity, not nameplate — a 1-hour battery derates heavily because a stress event can outlast it; a 4-hour battery derates far less. Cleared at £27.10/kW/year for 2029/30.
BM
Balancing Mechanism. The market the system operator uses in the final hour before real time to match supply and demand exactly. Now one of the largest battery revenue lines, because batteries respond in seconds and can both absorb and inject power.
Ancillary services
Products bought to keep the grid stable second by second — mainly frequency response. They fell from 87% of the battery revenue stack in 2020–22 to about 33% today, because the requirement is fixed and the fleet grew into it. Any revenue backed by a fixed-volume requirement gets competed away.
Tolling agreement
A counterparty pays a fixed fee for the exclusive right to operate the asset and take all its market revenue. The owner gives up upside and receives predictability — which, as section 2 shows, buys cheaper leverage.
CFADS
Cash Flow Available for Debt Service. Revenue, less operating costs, tax, working capital movements and maintenance capital expenditure. It is not EBITDA — using EBITDA in its place is one of the most common and most costly errors an engineer makes when first building a project model.
DSCR
Debt Service Cover Ratio = CFADS ÷ debt service. The central covenant of project finance. Roughly 1.20–1.30× for contracted renewables; 1.60× or more for merchant batteries, if financeable at all. The ratio is the price list for risk.
Debt sculpting
Deriving the repayment schedule from the cash flow so DSCR stays constant, rather than imposing a flat schedule. Debt service = CFADS ÷ target DSCR, and maximum debt is the present value of that stream.
Merchant
Exposed to market prices with no contract. The merchant tail is the part of an asset's life after its subsidy ends — simultaneously the least certain and often the largest component of value, which is why two credible buyers can differ by 30% on price for the same asset.
TNUoS
Transmission Network Use of System. The charge for using the high-voltage network, and it is locational — a northern Scottish generator pays far more than a southern English one. Averaging £13.03/kW for 2026/27, and charged on capacity, so it does not fall in a low-wind year.
P50 / P90
Production levels exceeded 50% and 90% of the time. Lenders size debt on P90, not P50. P90 = P50 × (1 − 1.2816 σ), where σ is the combined uncertainty. Every one percentage point of uncertainty you remove is worth roughly 1.28% of debt capacity — which is how energy yield analysis becomes, directly, a financial input.
