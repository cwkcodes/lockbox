## 1. Positioning

### The statement you proposed

> *"An energy investment and commercial professional with strong engineering,
> technical-due-diligence and energy-modelling foundations."*

**Problem:** it claims the destination before you have arrived, and a reader who checks your
CV will see a consultant. Claiming a title you cannot yet evidence reads as aspiration and
costs credibility.

### Improved positioning statement

> **"I assess whether energy assets will deliver the cash flows investors are underwriting —
> combining wind, solar and storage engineering with the financial modelling that determines
> what they are worth."**

**Why this is stronger:** it describes what you *do*, which you can evidence today; it is
oriented to the investor's problem rather than your job title; it names the specific
technologies; and it claims the financial modelling as an extension of the engineering rather
than as a separate, unproven skill. It is true now and becomes more true as the programme
progresses.

### Longer professional summary

> Renewable energy engineer specialising in the technical assessment of energy assets for
> investors and lenders. Background in wind, solar, storage and hydro across feasibility,
> energy yield assessment (MCP, P50/P90), technical due diligence, grid connection and
> repowering. Increasingly focused on the commercial and financial side of energy
> transactions: project finance structuring, revenue modelling under merchant and contracted
> arrangements, and the capture-price and constraint dynamics that determine what GB
> renewable assets actually earn. Builds Python and Excel models for market analysis and
> investment appraisal.

**Update this every quarter as the programme progresses.** It should always be *just ahead*
of where you are, never more than one step ahead.

### CV strategy

- **Reframe every bullet from task to commercial consequence.** Not "conducted energy yield
  assessments" but "produced P50/P90 energy yield assessments used to size debt on £Xm of
  project financings". Same work, different reader.
- Lead with transaction-adjacent work: technical due diligence, options appraisals,
  acquisition support, lender's technical adviser mandates.
- Quantify: MW assessed, £ of transaction value supported, number of deals.
- Add a **Selected Projects** section linking the portfolio repository.
- Add a **Technical Skills** line: financial modelling, project finance, Python (pandas,
  optimisation), Excel, QGIS, energy yield software.
- **Do not** claim finance experience you lack. State the modelling you have genuinely built.

### LinkedIn strategy

Headline: the positioning statement, trimmed. Not "Renewable Energy Consultant at Locogen" —
that is a job title, not a position.

**Post cadence: one substantive post per month, no more.** Content = what you are learning,
shown as analysis. Your Project 1 finding — GB solar driving the peak/off-peak spread negative
— is exactly the right kind of post: a real chart, a real dataset, a non-obvious observation, a
stated limitation. **[OPINION]** Ten such posts over a year will do more for your network than
500 connection requests, because they demonstrate rather than assert.

Do not post motivational content, career-change narratives, or "excited to announce". Post
analysis.

## 2. Networking — specific, not "network more"

### Who to contact, in priority order

| Priority | Group | Where to find them | The specific ask |
|:---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Colleagues staffing TDD/LTA work** | Internal | "How do I get onto transaction mandates?" |
| 2 | **Lender's technical advisers** | DNV, Wood Mackenzie, AFRY, TNEI, Everoze, Natural Power | "How does an LTA report get used by the credit committee?" |
| 3 | **Infrastructure debt professionals** | Bank and fund infra teams; IJGlobal deal announcements | "What do you wish technical advisers understood about how you use their reports?" |
| 4 | **Renewable PE / fund technical teams** | Greencoat, Foresight, Octopus, Quinbrook, Equitix | "How do technical people move onto the investment side?" |
| 5 | **BESS optimisers** | Habitat, Flexitricity (Edinburgh), Modo, Arenko, Field | "How do you handle degradation in dispatch optimisation?" |
| 6 | **Developer commercial teams** | RWE, ScottishPower, SSE, Statkraft, Fred. Olsen | "How does route-to-market decision-making work?" |
| 7 | **Ex-engineers now in finance/trading** | LinkedIn search: previous role engineer + current role investment | "What was the hardest part of the transition?" |
| 8 | **Data-centre energy teams** | Equinix, Digital Realty, Vantage; hyperscaler energy teams | "How do you evaluate a constrained connection?" |
| 9 | **Specialist recruiters** | Arkesden, Dartmouth, Circle Square, Selby Jennings, HC Group | "What does the market pay, and what am I missing?" |

**Volume: 2–3 conversations per week, sustained.** That is 100–150 per year. Do not do 20 in
one week and then none for three months.

### How to reach out — the actual template

Short, specific, no ask beyond time, one clear reason why *them*:

> Hi [Name] — I'm a renewable energy engineer in Edinburgh doing technical due diligence on
> wind and solar assets, and I'm working towards the investment side. I built a GB power
> market model that measures capture rates from Elexon settlement data — it showed solar
> capturing 82% of baseload this summer, which surprised me.
>
> I saw you work on [specific thing]. Would you have 20 minutes to tell me how [specific
> question]? Happy to share the analysis if it's useful.

**Why this works:** it demonstrates rather than asserts, it gives before it asks, the ask is
small and bounded, and it names something specific about them. **Response rates on this kind
of message are far higher than on generic requests** because you have already proved you are
worth 20 minutes.

### What to ask

Good: how work actually gets allocated · what separates good technical advisers from poor
ones · which skills they hire for and cannot find · how deals actually get sourced · what
they wish they had learned earlier · what a typical week looks like · **compensation
*structure*** — base/bonus split, whether carry is offered, when it vests.

**Bad, and will end the relationship:** "can you get me a job" · "what do you earn" · "can
you review my CV" (as a first ask) · anything you could have found on their website ·
anything that treats them as a means to an end.

### Follow-up

Within 24 hours: thank them, and reference one specific thing you will act on. Then — and
this is the part almost nobody does — **come back in 6–8 weeks with the result.** "You
suggested I look at how capture rates vary seasonally; I ran it over 12 months and found X."
That single follow-up converts a contact into a relationship, because it proves you listen
and execute.

Locogen already sells technical due diligence "for buyers, sellers and lenders" and lender's
technical adviser services, and has a dedicated Financial Investors sector, having supported
transactions including Severn Trent Green Power's acquisition of up to four solar farms

**You do not need to leave to get transaction exposure. You need to be staffed on it.**

### Volunteer for these specifically, in this order

1. **Lender's technical adviser mandates** — highest value. You see the financial model, the
   credit process and the lender's real concerns.
2. **Transaction technical due diligence** — buy-side or sell-side.
3. **Revenue and yield assessment for acquisitions** — bridges your existing skill directly
   into valuation.
4. **PPA and offtake contract review** — commercial contract literacy.
5. **Grid risk assessment for transactions** — Gate 2 status diligence is live, scarce work
   right now [FACT: TMO4+ reform].
6. **Portfolio valuation and asset performance analysis**.
7. **Investment committee support** for clients.

### The conversation to have with your manager

Do not ask "can I do more finance work". Propose something concrete:

> "I've noticed we do a lot of LTA and TDD work for the financial investor clients. I'd like
> to specialise in that side. I've been building the financial modelling skills to go with the
> technical work — here's a GB market model I built. Could I be staffed on the next
> transaction mandate that comes in, and shadow the commercial side of it?"

**Why this framing works:** it is a proposal to be more useful to the business, not a request
for a favour. It is aligned with a service line the firm already sells. And it comes with
evidence attached.

**If the answer is no**, that is genuinely valuable information — it means the internal bridge
is closed and the timeline shifts to an external move. Find that out at day 90, not year two.

## 4. Interview preparation

### Technical topics you will be asked

Capture price and cannibalisation (**know this cold — it is the standard screening question
for renewables investment roles**) · P50/P90 and why lenders use P90 · curtailment and
constraints in GB · why LCOE is the wrong investment metric · battery revenue stacking and
degradation · clean spark spread and dispatch · grid connection and Gate 2 · CfD mechanics
including the price base year and CPI indexation.

### Financial modelling topics

Debt sizing and sculpting · DSCR, LLCR, and what the covenant does when breached · the cash
waterfall order · circularity and how you resolve it · real vs nominal · WACC construction ·
why merchant and contracted cash flows get different discount rates · tax depreciation vs book
depreciation.

**Expect a live modelling test** for investment and project finance roles. Typically 2–3 hours,
building a simplified project model from a blank workbook. **This is pass/fail and it is
practised, not improvised.** Build Project 2 twice and time yourself.

### Investment case topics

"Walk me through how you'd value this wind farm" · "What would make you walk away?" · "What's
the biggest risk and how would you mitigate it?" · "Where do you disagree with the seller's
case?" · "What's your view on GB power prices to 2035, and why should I believe you?"

**The meta-skill:** always state your assumptions before your answer, and always say what
would change your mind. Investment professionals are trained to probe for false confidence.
Displaying calibrated uncertainty is a positive signal, not a weak one.

### Trading interview topics (if pursued)

Mental arithmetic under time pressure · probability and expected value · forward curve
interpretation · "what's your edge?" · position sizing · how you handle being wrong.
**Trading interviews test temperament more than knowledge** — specifically, whether you can
change your mind quickly when the evidence changes, without ego.

### Your three networking talking points

1. **"GB solar has driven the peak/off-peak spread negative this summer — peak PPA structures
   are pricing a shape that no longer exists."** Real, measured from settlement data, and
   most people have not noticed.
2. **"Capture rate is a better screening metric than LCOE, and almost nobody models it
   properly."** True, and it demonstrates you think like an investor.
3. **"The Gate 2 queue reform has made grid connection status the scarcest asset in GB
   development."** Current, specific, and directly connected to work you can do.

Each is a genuine observation, not a pitch. **[OPINION]** Having three of these is worth more
than any elevator pitch, because they start conversations among peers rather than requests
from a supplicant.
