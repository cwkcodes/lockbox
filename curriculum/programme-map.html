Where you actually stand

Further along on analysis than you think. Further behind on capital.

You already do a junior version of the job

Technical due diligence, P50/P90 yield work, grid screening and NPV analysis are not adjacent to infrastructure investment — they are inputs to it. The gap is that you produce an input to a decision rather than making the decision, and you are paid for hours rather than outcomes.

Your employer is already in the room

Locogen sells technical due diligence to buyers, sellers and lenders, runs lender's technical adviser mandates, and has a dedicated financial-investor client sector. You do not need to leave to get transaction exposure. You need to be staffed on it.

The constraint is fluency, not knowledge

You can calculate an NPV. You cannot yet sculpt debt to a DSCR covenant, defend a capture-rate assumption to a credit committee, or read an SPA for risk transfer. That is 12–24 months of work — and it is not learnable by reading.

The energy system, in five layers

Every physical event has a price consequence, and every price consequence has an investment consequence. Tracing that path in both directions is the skill this programme exists to build.

Layer 1 — physical flows and where value concentrates

What it shows. Energy and materials flowing left to right, losing usable energy at every conversion. What to take from it. The system has few genuine chokepoints, and value concentrates at them rather than at the resource. Wind is free; a grid connection in a constrained region is not. This is the most important commercial idea in energy, and it is why the reformed GB connections queue has made a Gate 2 offer a scarce, tradeable asset.

Diagram: original, constructed for this programme. Structure follows the IEA energy-balance convention (primary → transformation → final consumption). Not to scale; thickness indicates relative importance, not measured flow.

Layer 3 — the money layer, and why this diagram decides your career

Follow the money to the right-hand edge. Limited partners supply the capital and take roughly 80% of profit above a hurdle. The general partner supplies judgement and takes roughly 20% — on capital that is mostly not theirs. That asymmetry is the entire economic reason to move from consulting into investment, and every career route in this programme is an argument about how to reach the right-hand side, at what risk. Note also where risk stops: the SPV is engineered so that project failure does not reach the sponsor's other assets. That is what non-recourse means, and why project finance is a distinct discipline.

Diagram: original. Carry percentages are the conventional 2-and-20 structure; actual terms vary by fund and are negotiated with LPs. Carry vests over years and is worth zero if the fund misses its hurdle — never model it as salary.

05

The interactive one: GB price shape

Real Elexon settlement data, pulled 25 August 2026. This chart contains a live commercial finding, and the control below demonstrates it.

GB day-ahead price by hour of day, 26 July – 25 August 2026

FACT — real market data
Peak window

What changed, and why it matters. Under the classic 07:00–19:00 peak definition the spread is negative — peak power is cheaper than off-peak. Switch to an evening window and the same data produces a large positive spread. Nothing about the market changed; only the definition did. GB summer solar has carved a midday trough to about £88/MWh while the evening ramp reaches £164/MWh, and the traditional peak window now contains the trough it was designed to exclude. Any PPA still priced on a 07:00–19:00 peak premium is pricing a shape that no longer exists.

The commercial consequences. Solar captured 81.9% of baseload over this window and wind 94.0% — cannibalisation, measured rather than assumed. Apply a flat baseload price to solar volume and you overstate revenue by roughly 18% before any curtailment. Meanwhile gas captured 115.5% and nuclear 103.2%: dispatchable plant earns above average precisely because intermittent plant earns below it. And the £88-to-£164 evening ramp, not the £123 average, is what a battery monetises.

Source: Elexon BMRS dataset MID, provider APXMIDP (N2EX day-ahead index) · Great Britain · 26 Jul – 25 Aug 2026 · 1,441 half-hourly settlement periods · £/MWh · retrieved 25 Aug 2026. Hourly values are means across all days; the −£1.09/MWh headline figure quoted elsewhere in the programme uses a weekday-only peak definition, which is why the figures here differ. 30 days is a short and seasonally atypical window — do not generalise these capture rates to an annual model. Fully reproducible: curriculum/projects/p1_gb_power_dashboard/ (Python, no API key required).

Use this

This finding is your best networking opening. It is real, it is current, it is measured from primary settlement data, and most people in the industry have not noticed it. It demonstrates commercial judgement rather than asserting it — which is precisely what a cold approach to an investor needs to do.

Curriculum dependencies

Twenty-two module groups. Two chains carry almost all the value.

What must be learned before what

Order matters more than coverage. Accounting comes before project finance because you cannot sculpt debt against cash flow without knowing how CFADS differs from EBITDA — engineers routinely skip this and then build models with the tax line wrong. Power markets come before everything commercial because every revenue assumption in every energy model traces back to how the price forms. Data centres come late but not last: the module is only credible once you can discuss capacity markets, connection constraints and backup generation.

07

The first ninety days

At ten hours per week. The gates matter more than the modules — the dominant failure mode is drift, not difficulty.

  • Week 1

    Module A1, and the conversation

    Energy, power and units. Build the LCOE and marginal-cost workbook. In the same week, ask your line manager to be staffed on lender's technical adviser and transaction due-diligence mandates. Frame it as a proposal to be more useful, with the market model attached as evidence — not as a request for a favour.

  • Weeks 2–4

    Finish A1, start the market work

    Complete the quiz and the Scottish Borders valuation case. Register for Elexon data access. Begin Module B. Book the first two informational conversations. Request two recruiter salary guides — public aggregator data for these roles is unreliable and you need better numbers.

  • Day 30 — gate

    Did the internal conversation happen?

    Not "did it succeed" — did it happen. If you completed thirty of forty planned hours and had the conversation, continue. If you avoided the conversation, that is the real finding, and it is the thing to fix.

  • Weeks 5–8

    Market fluency

    Price formation, merit order, balancing and imbalance settlement, capacity mechanisms. Master capture price, cannibalisation and the clean spark spread. Three conversations: a technical adviser, an infrastructure debt or fund professional, a battery optimiser. Project 1 pulling live data and producing four charts.

  • Weeks 9–13

    Ship, and reposition

    Route-to-market, PPAs, CfDs, capture price. Start accounting in parallel — it uses different mental energy from market work. Book the project-finance modelling course for month four; it is the one paid course worth buying. Publish Project 1. Rewrite LinkedIn around what you assess, not what you are called.

  • Day 90 — gate

    Has your work content changed at all?

    If you have been staffed on transaction work, the primary route is validated. If the conversation went nowhere, the internal bridge is closed and the plan shifts towards an external move at month twelve to eighteen. Either answer is useful. Discovering it at day ninety rather than year two is the entire point of the gate.

Do not apply for anything yet

No applications before day ninety, deliberately. You get one first impression with each employer, and sending it before Project 1 exists wastes it. Five targeted applications after the portfolio is live will outperform fifty sent before.

08

How engineering becomes ownership

The transition is not a leap. It is four steps, each of which uses the previous one as evidence.

Step 2 is the one that matters and the one people skip. It requires no job change, no relocation, no pay cut and no credential — only being staffed on work your employer already sells to financial investors. It is also where you first see how a lender actually uses the technical report you have been writing for years, which reframes everything that follows. The failure mode is benign: stopping at step 2 makes you a well-paid senior technical adviser in Edinburgh. That floor is what makes taking risk beyond it rational.

The dominant risk is not failure — it is drift. Completing modules, accumulating knowledge, and never converting any of it into a different seat. Every gate in the ninety-day plan exists to counter that specific failure. And the downside is asymmetric in your favour: attempt the ambitious path and land in the central case, and you have roughly £2m. Attempt the central case and land in the conservative one, and you have a good career in Scotland and roughly £890k. The cost of trying is small; the upside is large. That asymmetry, not optimism, is the rational case for the programme.
