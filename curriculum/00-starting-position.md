# Part 2 — Starting Position and Competency Assessment

## 1. Concise assessment of where you are

**[OPINION, evidence-based]** You are further along than you think on *analysis*, and
further behind than you think on *capital*.

Three observations that shape everything else in this programme:

**a) You already do a junior version of the job you say you want.**
Technical due diligence, options appraisals, energy yield P50/P90 work, grid screening
and NPV/IRR analysis are not adjacent to infrastructure investment — they *are* inputs
to it. When an infrastructure fund buys a wind farm, someone writes the technical
section of the investment committee paper. Your work is the raw material for that
section. The gap is not knowledge of energy. It is that you produce an input to a
decision rather than making the decision, and you are paid a day rate for hours rather
than a share of an outcome.

**b) Your employer is already in the room.** Locogen explicitly markets technical due
diligence "for buyers, sellers and lenders" and lender's technical advisor services,
and has a dedicated "Financial Investors" client sector; it has advised on transactions
including Severn Trent Green Power's acquisition of up to four solar farms
([FACT] — [Locogen: Renewable Energy Advisory](https://locogen.com/solution/renewable-energy-advisory/),
[Locogen: Financial Investors](https://locogen.com/sector/financial-investors/),
[Locogen/Severn Trent](https://locogen.com/locogen-severntrent-greenpower-4-solar-farms/),
accessed 25 Aug 2026). This is the single most important fact in your assessment. You do
not need to leave to get transaction exposure. You need to get staffed on LTA and TDD
mandates, sit on the calls with the fund's investment team, and read the models those
funds are running. That is a conversation with your line manager, not a career change.

**c) The binding constraint is financial fluency, not financial knowledge.**
You can already calculate an NPV. What you cannot yet do — and what separates you from
an infrastructure investment associate — is: size and sculpt project debt to a DSCR
covenant, build a three-statement operating model, argue about whether a merchant tail
should be discounted at 8% or 11%, defend a capture-rate assumption to a credit
committee, or read a share purchase agreement and identify which risks actually sit with
the buyer. These are learnable in 12–24 months. They are not learnable by reading.

### The uncomfortable parts

- **£200k+ is achievable on several routes but is a 5–10 year outcome, not a 2-year one**,
  and on some routes it is a genuine tail outcome rather than a median one. Route-by-route
  probabilities are in `03-career-routes.md`. Treat any route that claims otherwise as
  mis-sold.
- **Age ~30 is fine but not early.** For infrastructure PE and investment banking, the
  conventional entry is 22–26 via graduate schemes. You will enter laterally, on the
  strength of sector expertise, into an associate-equivalent seat — or not at all. That is
  a narrower door but the people who get through it are paid for something graduates do
  not have.
- **Scotland is a real constraint for some routes and irrelevant for others.** Edinburgh
  and Glasgow have genuine renewable investment, developer and asset-management activity.
  They have almost no commodity trading. Physical energy trading is London, Geneva,
  Houston, Singapore. If trading is the goal, relocation is not optional.
- **The New Jersey / US north-east possibility is a strategic asset, not a disruption** —
  PJM is the most commercially interesting power market in the world right now because of
  data-centre load growth, and its capacity auction has cleared at the price cap two years
  running ([FACT], see `01-energy-system-map.md`, chain 5). Plan around it deliberately.

### Category detail

For each: what transfers · what is missing · commercial standard · specialist standard ·
fastest route to close.

**3. Electrical power systems** — *Transfers:* grid-connection screening, three-phase
basics, energy-flow modelling. *Missing:* reactive power economics, fault level and
short-circuit strength as *commercial* constraints, why inertia is now a traded product.
*Commercial standard:* explain to an investment committee why a connection offer's fault
level headroom kills a battery project. *Specialist:* run load flow studies. *You need
commercial only.* → Module Group C, 20 hrs.

**5. Conventional generation** — *Transfers:* thermodynamics, efficiency, heat transfer.
*Missing:* heat rate as a commercial variable, start costs, minimum stable generation,
spark/dark spreads. *Commercial standard:* compute a clean spark spread and state whether a
CCGT dispatches. *Specialist:* plant-level optimisation. *You need commercial.*
→ Module Group F + Project 4, 30 hrs. **This gap is larger than you expect and matters more
than you expect** — thermal assets are where most energy P&L is made.

**9. Energy markets** — *Transfers:* you know what a CfD and a PPA are. *Missing:* how
prices actually form half-hour by half-hour; imbalance settlement; what a capture price
really measures; cannibalisation mechanics. *Commercial standard:* explain why a Scottish
wind farm's capture price diverges from baseload and quantify it. *Specialist:* price
formation modelling. *You need deep.* → Module Group B + Project 1, 60 hrs.

**11. Trading and hedging** — *Transfers:* essentially nothing. Probability and
distributions from P50/P90 work help with the maths, not the instinct. *Missing:*
everything — forward curves, optionality, Greeks, VaR, mark-to-market. *Commercial
standard:* read a forward curve, explain a hedge, compute a futures P&L. *Specialist:* run
a book. *You need working knowledge unless you choose the trading route, in which case
deep.* → Module Group P + Project 10, 50 hrs working / 200+ hrs specialist.

**14. Project finance** — *Transfers:* CAPEX/OPEX/NPV/IRR, P50/P90 — genuinely useful, and
P50/P90 is *directly* the debt-sizing input (lenders size to P90). *Missing:* debt sizing,
sculpting, DSCR/LLCR covenants, cash waterfalls, security packages, the entire legal
architecture. *Commercial standard:* build a sculpted-debt project model from blank and
defend the DSCR. *Specialist:* structure and negotiate the facility. *You need deep.*
→ Module Group R + Project 2, 80 hrs. **Highest priority in the programme.**

**22. Excel** — *Transfers:* you build calculation tools. *Missing:* institutional
financial modelling discipline — flags, corkscrews, circularity handling, error checks,
FAST/SMART standards. An engineer's spreadsheet and a banker's model differ in *structure*,
not difficulty. This is a fast, high-leverage gap. → Module Group R practicals, 25 hrs.

**25. Capital allocation** — *Transfers:* options appraisal is a primitive form of it.
*Missing:* thinking in terms of risk-adjusted return on capital deployed, opportunity cost,
hurdle rates, portfolio construction. *This is the actual job* at the senior end of every
route on your list. Closed by doing, not reading — Projects 9 and 2.

### What you do NOT need to learn deeply

Explicitly de-prioritised. Awareness only:

- Petroleum geology and reservoir engineering (Group G) — know the vocabulary and decline
  curves; do not learn to log a well.
- Reactor physics (Group J) — nuclear's risks are political, construction and financial, not
  physics.
- Protection engineering, relay coordination (Group C).
- Refinery process engineering detail (Group I) — learn crack spreads, not hydrotreater design.
- Battery electrochemistry below cell level (Group E) — degradation *behaviour* matters; cell
  chemistry does not.
- Mining metallurgy (Group M).
- Accounting to preparer standard (Group Q) — you must *read* accounts, not produce them. No
  ACA/ACCA.
- Derivatives pricing mathematics (Group P) — unless you take the quant route, understand
  what delta and vega *mean*, not how to derive Black-Scholes.

for your purposes. Treating all of it as deep study is the most likely way this programme
fails.
