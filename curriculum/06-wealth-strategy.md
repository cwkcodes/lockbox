## 2. Why earnings beat investing returns — for you, at this stage

The most important arithmetic in this file.

**[CALC]** Suppose you have £50,000 invested and earn £60,000.

- **Outperforming the market by 2%/yr** on £50k = **£1,000/yr**. Achieving this consistently
  is close to impossible; most professional funds fail to.
- **Increasing income by 20%** = **£12,000/yr**, before compounding into future raises.

**The income lever is 12× larger and far more controllable.** It stays larger until your
invested capital reaches roughly 5–10× your annual income — realistically your mid-40s.

**Conclusion:** for the next decade your investing strategy should be *boring and automatic*
so that all your discretionary attention goes into human capital and career transition. Any
hour spent picking stocks is an hour not spent on Module R, and Module R has a far higher
expected return.

## 3. Concentration risk — the specific trap in your situation

You are about to become *more* concentrated in energy, not less:

- Your **salary** depends on the energy sector.
- Your **bonus** depends on energy asset performance.
- Your **carry or project equity** would depend on specific energy assets.
- If you also hold energy shares, **every source of wealth correlates**.

A sector downturn would hit your job, your bonus, your carry and your portfolio
simultaneously — exactly when you would need liquidity.

**[OPINION] Therefore: your personal portfolio should be deliberately under-weight energy,
possibly holding none at all.** This feels wrong — energy is what you know — and that
feeling is the bias. Your *human capital* is already a leveraged, undiversified bet on the
energy sector. The personal portfolio's job is to hedge it, not to double it.

**Practical constraint to plan for:** many investment and trading employers impose **personal
account dealing restrictions** — pre-clearance, minimum holding periods, restricted lists,
outright bans on sector securities. Broad index funds are almost always permitted; individual
energy shares often are not. **Build the habit of index investing now**, before an employer
compels it.

## 4. The hierarchy — do these in order

1. **Emergency fund: 6 months' essential expenses**, in cash, instant access. Non-negotiable
   before anything else. It is what lets you take a lateral pay cut for deal exposure, or
   leave a bad role. **The emergency fund is a career asset, not just a financial one.**
2. **Clear high-interest debt.** Paying off debt at 20% is a guaranteed 20% return, tax-free.
   Nothing in markets beats that risk-adjusted.
3. **Pension to at least the full employer match.** An employer match is an instant 50–100%
   return. Not taking it is leaving cash on the table.
4. **Stocks and Shares ISA** — £20,000 annual allowance (verify current limit). Tax-free
   growth and withdrawals, no lock-up. **This is your primary vehicle**, because liquidity
   matters when you may want to fund a co-investment or a business.
5. **Additional pension contributions** — particularly powerful above the higher-rate
   threshold, and especially in the £100k–£125,140 band where the personal allowance taper
   creates an effective marginal rate around 60%. **[FACT: HMRC — verify current thresholds.]**
   If your income enters that band, pension contributions are the highest-return financial
   action available to you.
6. **General investment account** once ISA and pension are exhausted.
7. **Only then**: property, co-investment, direct project equity.

## 5. Saving, investing, trading, owning — four different activities

| | Expected return | Risk | Time | Verdict for you |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Saving** | ≈ inflation | Very low | None | Emergency fund only |
| **Investing** | Equity risk premium | Moderate, diversifiable | ~1 hr/month | **Core. Automate it.** |
| **Trading** | ≈0 or negative retail | High | Enormous | **Avoid personally.** Learn it professionally, where you use someone else's capital and get paid for the option |
| **Owning** | Highest | Concentrated, illiquid | Enormous | **The multi-million route** |

**The distinction that matters:** professional trading and retail trading are different
activities. In a trading role you deploy institutional capital, with risk limits, information
advantages and a payoff structure where you keep a share of gains and do not personally fund
losses. That is an attractive asymmetry. Replicating it in a personal account is not — you
have none of the advantages and all of the downside.

## 6. Where multi-million net worth actually comes from

**[OPINION], stated plainly.** Salary alone will not do it. £150k gross in Scotland is
roughly £95–100k net; saving 30% is ~£30k/yr; over 15 years at 5% real that is roughly
**£680k** — a good outcome, not a multi-million one.

The multi-million outcomes come from **ownership**:

- **Carried interest** — 20% of profits above a hurdle on a fund you help manage. A single
  successful fund cycle can produce a seven-figure carry payment for a senior professional.
  **[ESTIMATE]** Realistic for you in years 8–15 *if* the infrastructure PE route works.
  Note carry vests slowly and is worthless if the fund misses its hurdle.
- **Development equity** — retaining 5–20% of a project you originate. A 50 MW BESS or wind
  project sold at FID can be worth several million; a 10% retained stake is life-changing.
  **[ESTIMATE]** Realistic in years 5–15 via the developer or entrepreneurial route, and it
  is the reason route 3 and the entrepreneurial route appear in the recommendation despite
  their variance.
- **Business equity** — building and selling a company.

All three require you to be on the **right-hand side of the money diagram** in
`01-energy-system-map.md` Layer 3. That is the strategic point of this entire programme.

### How to read these three tables

The gap between Conservative (£890k) and Ambitious (£4.7m) at year 15 is **not mostly
investment returns** — the same 5% real return is assumed in all three. It is almost entirely
**earned income and ownership**. That is the argument of §2 and §6, quantified.

**The failure mode is benign.** If you attempt Ambitious and land in Central, you have ~£2m.
If you attempt Central and land in Conservative, you have ~£890k and a good career in
Scotland. **The downside of trying is small; the upside is large.** That asymmetry is the
rational case for the whole programme.

## 8. Practical actions in the first 90 days

1. Calculate your actual current position — replace the [ASSUMPTION] starting figures above
   with real numbers. **Do this first; the scenarios are meaningless without it.**
2. Confirm the emergency fund is at 6 months. If not, fill it before increasing investment.
3. Confirm you are capturing the full employer pension match.
4. Set up a monthly automatic ISA contribution into a globally diversified, low-cost index
   fund. Automate it so it needs no decisions.
5. Check your pension's fund selection — default funds are often more conservative than
   appropriate for a 30-year horizon.
6. Record your baseline net worth. Update annually, not monthly. **[OPINION]** Checking more
   often produces anxiety, not returns.
7. Read your employment contract for any existing personal-account-dealing restrictions.
