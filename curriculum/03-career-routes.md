## 0. Read this before any number in this file

**Public salary data for these roles is bad.** You asked me not to invent salary ranges, so
I must be explicit about what the available sources actually support.

The problem is title conflation. Glassdoor reports "Infrastructure and Project Finance
Associate" in London at an average of **£40,566**, range £28,468–£60,540, 90th percentile
£91,644, from 1,528 submissions as of June 2026
([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Salaries/london-infrastructure-and-project-finance-associate-salary-SRCH_KO0,44.htm)).
That figure is **not** the front-office project finance associate role discussed here — it
pools corporate finance, back-office and support roles carrying similar titles. A genuine
project finance associate at a European bank in London is paid materially more than £40k.

Meanwhile Glassdoor reports "Private Equity Associate" in London averaging **£151,758**
(£99,418–£235,680)
([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Salaries/london-private-equity-associate-salary-SRCH_IL.0,6_IM1035_KO7,31.htm)),
and Canary Wharfian's 2026 guide puts PE associates at 3–5 years' experience on
**£160,000–£370,000 total cash**
([Canary Wharfian](https://www.canarywharfian.co.uk/private-equity/639/private-equity-salary-guide-2026-in-londoneurope)) —
but those skew to large-cap **buyout** PE, which pays more than infrastructure PE.

**So:** every figure below is tagged. **[FACT]** = directly cited. **[ESTIMATE]** = my
judgement, triangulated from cited sources plus role structure, and explicitly not measured.
Where I have no defensible basis, I say "insufficient public data" rather than inventing a
range.

**How to get real numbers — do this in the first 90 days.** (a) [HC Group's compensation
reports](https://www.hcgroup.global/reports) for trading; (b) recruiter salary guides from
Arkesden, Dartmouth Partners, Circle Square, Selby Jennings — request directly, they are
free; (c) live job ads with disclosed ranges (increasingly common); (d) three informational
conversations per route, where you ask about *structure* — base/bonus split, carry
participation — not about individuals' pay. Structure questions get answered; level
questions do not.

**Market context [FACT, HC Group, 25 Feb 2026]:** bonus pools moderated across most
commodity markets during 2025 on more normalised returns; the market has shifted "from
candidate-led to employer-led", with sign-on bonuses harder to secure and guarantees more
limited. Premium demand persists in **LNG optimisation, short-term power in growth markets,
analytics-enabled trading, and structured origination**
([HC Group](https://www.hcgroup.global/insights/talent-trends/hc-talent-intelligence-2026-outlook-compensation-trends-in-energy-trading)).
Note that three of those four premium areas reward exactly the analytical profile you have.

## 2. Deep profiles — the seven routes that matter for you

### Route 2 — Lender's Technical Adviser → Infrastructure Debt ★ highest risk-adjusted

**What the work actually is.** An LTA is retained by lenders to tell them whether a project
will physically produce the cash flows the financial model assumes. You review the energy
yield, the EPC and O&M contracts, the grid connection, the technology's track record, the
construction programme, and you sign a report the credit committee relies on. During
operations you certify milestones and drawdowns.

**Daily activities:** reading data rooms; challenging the sponsor's yield consultant;
reconciling the technical case to the financial model's P90; drafting red-flag reports;
calls with the bank's deal team, the sponsor and lawyers; site visits.

**Titles:** Technical Adviser → Senior Technical Adviser → Technical Director. Then the
crossover: Infrastructure Debt Analyst/Associate → Investment Manager → Director.

**Employers:** DNV, Wood Mackenzie, Arup, AFRY, TNEI, Everoze, Natural Power, BDO/Deloitte
infrastructure teams — **and Locogen, which already sells this service**
([FACT](https://locogen.com/solution/renewable-energy-advisory/)). Then on the debt side:
Allianz GI, AXA IM Alts, Macquarie AM, NatWest, Santander, Rabobank, SMBC, MUFG, Triodos.

**How your experience transfers:** almost completely. Energy yield assessment, MCP, Weibull,
P50/P90, technical DD, grid screening and repowering studies *are* the LTA toolkit. **You
are already qualified for the first half of this route.**

**Your largest gap:** the debt side. You can say a P90 is 82% of P50; you cannot yet sculpt
a debt profile so that DSCR ≥ 1.30x on the P90 case, or argue about a 6-month tail. That is
Module Group R.

**Realistic entry route — and this is the point:**
1. Ask to be staffed on Locogen's LTA and TDD mandates. **This week.**
2. Do 3–5 of them. Ask each time for the sponsor's or lender's financial model.
3. Rebuild that model yourself from blank (Project 2). Compare.
4. Move to a specialist TA firm as Senior TA, *or* apply directly to infra debt funds
   pitching "I have signed off the technical case on £X of financed assets".

**Hours:** 40–50/wk, deal-driven spikes. **Travel:** moderate; site visits.
**Cyclicality:** low — debt gets deployed in most conditions. **Job security:** high.

**Compensation.** UK TA roles: **[ESTIMATE] £45–75k** at consultant level, £80–120k at
director level — triangulated from UK engineering consultancy structures, not from a
specific citation; verify with recruiter guides. Infrastructure debt investment
professionals sit well above the conflated Glassdoor figure discussed in §0;
**[ESTIMATE] £150–350k** total at senior level, with limited or no carry (debt funds pay
less carry than equity funds because returns are lower). **Insufficient public data for a
defensible precise range — this is a priority question for your informational conversations.**

**Fit 8 · Risk-adjusted 9.** The highest risk-adjusted score in the table, because the first
three steps require no job change, no relocation, no pay cut and no credential. The downside
if you stop halfway is that you become a well-paid senior technical adviser in Edinburgh —
which is route 14, a perfectly good outcome.

**First five actions:** (1) internal conversation about LTA staffing; (2) Module Group R
started; (3) build Project 2; (4) request a recruiter salary guide covering infra debt;
(5) three informational calls with infra debt professionals.

### Route 1 — Renewable / Energy-Transition Infrastructure PE ★ highest ceiling with real odds

**What the work is.** Sourcing, diligencing, executing and managing equity investments in
energy assets and platforms. Building and owning the model. Writing IC papers. Negotiating
SPAs alongside lawyers. Sitting on asset boards post-close.

**Daily:** model iteration; reading TA/CDD/FDD reports (**the ones you currently write**);
IC memo drafting; management meetings; bid strategy; portfolio company calls.

**Employers:** Copenhagen Infrastructure Partners, Glennmont, Greencoat, Octopus Energy
Generation, Foresight, Quinbrook, Equitix, InfraRed, Actis, Macquarie GIG, Brookfield,
KKR infrastructure. Note RWE and KKR agreed a long-term partnership alongside RWE's 6.9 GW
AR7 award ([FACT](https://www.rwe.com/en/press/rwe-ag/2026-01-14-rwe-secures-contracts-for-difference-for-6-9-gigawatts-of-offshore-wind-capacity/)) —
these platform partnerships are where deal flow now sits.

**Locations:** London dominant; Edinburgh has genuine presence (Greencoat, Foresight and
several fund administrators); New York and Boston for the US north-east.

**How your experience transfers:** the technical diligence half, strongly. Funds
*consistently* under-staff genuine engineering judgement and over-staff modelling. Your
edge is being the person who says "that availability warranty does not cover what you think
it covers".

**Largest gaps:** modelling to institutional standard; the legal/SPA layer; and — honestly —
the absence of a banking or PE "stamp" on your CV, which is what the screening filter looks
for. You beat the filter with a portfolio, a referral, or a sector-specialist route in.

**Realistic entry:** *not* by applying cold to a generalist associate posting; you will lose
to an ex-banking analyst. Enter via (a) a fund's **asset management** or technical team, then
move across internally — the highest-probability path; (b) a developer's investment team, then
to the fund side; (c) LTA/TDD, becoming the person the fund already trusts, then being hired.

**Hours:** 55–70/wk, worse in live deals. **Travel:** moderate–high.
**Cyclicality:** moderate; fundraising cycles bite. **Job security:** moderate.

**Compensation.** Buyout PE associate benchmarks cited in §0 (£151,758 average; £160–370k
total cash at 3–5 years) are the **upper** reference; **infrastructure PE pays below buyout
PE** — **[ESTIMATE]** associate total cash £110–200k, VP £180–320k, Director/Principal
£300k–£600k+ including carry. **Carry is the real prize** and the real uncertainty: it vests
over years, depends on fund performance, and is worth zero if the fund underperforms its
hurdle. Do not model carry as salary.

**Fit 9 · Risk-adjusted 8. P(reaching £300k+) ≈ 15–25%** conditional on getting in at all —
and getting in at all is itself perhaps 30–50% likely on a 5-year serious effort from your
start point. **[ESTIMATE]** Compound those honestly: roughly a **1-in-7 to 1-in-10** chance
of the full outcome. That is a good bet, not a certainty.

### Route 4 — Data-Centre Energy Strategy ★ your sharpest differentiator

**What the work is.** Securing power for data centres: site selection against grid capacity,
connection strategy, PPA and carbon-free-energy procurement, on-site generation and storage,
backup and resilience design, and the total cost of energy. Sits between a hyperscaler /
colocation developer and the power system.

**Why this is your intersection — the honest argument.** The role demands mechanical
(cooling), electrical (power trains, UPS, resilience), renewable (PPAs, CFE matching), grid
(connections and constraints) and commercial (procurement, contracts) knowledge
*simultaneously*. Almost nobody has all five. You have four of them today and the fifth is
this programme. Pure finance candidates cannot do the engineering; pure data-centre engineers
cannot do the power procurement.

**The demand is documented, not speculative [FACT]:** IEA projects data-centre electricity
consumption roughly doubling from ~415 TWh (2024) to ~945–950 TWh by 2030
([IEA, *Energy and AI*](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai)),
and PJM's 2027/28 capacity auction cleared at the **$333.44/MW-day cap while still falling
6,623 MW short** of its reliability requirement
([PJM](https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-auction-procures-134479-mw-of-generation-resources/)).

**Employers:** hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, AWS, Meta) energy teams; colocation
developers (Equinix, Digital Realty, Vantage, CyrusOne, Ark); developers with data-centre
arms; specialist advisers.

**Location — and this is decisive.** Northern Virginia, PJM more broadly, Texas and Ohio are
the global centre of gravity. **Your possible New Jersey period puts you inside PJM.** If
that move happens, this route goes from "attractive" to "the obvious thing to do".

**Gaps:** data-centre-specific engineering (PUE, cooling topologies, Tier/Uptime resilience
standards, UPS architecture) — 40–60 hours, genuinely learnable; plus US market structure
(PJM interconnection queue, LMP, capacity market) — Module Group B comparative section.

**Compensation:** **insufficient reliable public UK data**; US roles are typically better
paid. **[ESTIMATE] £120–200k** UK senior; **$200–400k** US senior including equity.
Verify before relying on this.

**Fit 9 · Risk-adjusted 8.** **[OPINION]** This is the most *under-competed* route on the
list relative to demand growth, and the only one where a possible relocation is a strategic
advantage rather than a disruption.

Stay, become Principal/Technical Director, build the transaction advisory practice.

### Routes 20–22 — Gas / LNG / Oil Trading (the honest discouragement)

You listed these and I am going to argue against them, for you specifically.

Physical trading recruits overwhelmingly at graduate level into scheduling/operations, then
promotes internally over 5–10 years. Lateral entry at 30 without a trading background is
rare and usually happens through *analytics* or *origination*, not onto a desk. It requires
relocation to London, Geneva, Houston or Singapore. Compensation genuinely can exceed £1m —
and the aggregator data showing UK energy traders averaging **£61,475**
([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Salaries/energy-trader-salary-SRCH_KO0,13.htm))
with London mid-career total pay of **£65–85k**
([PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/UK/Job=Energy_Trader/Salary/99bdae47/Mid-Career-London))
is measuring the broad population, *not* the front-office prop desks that produce the
headline numbers. **Both facts are true simultaneously**: the median is unremarkable, the
tail is enormous, and the tail is what people talk about.

**[OPINION]** Oil trading (fit 3) is a poor fit: no transferable knowledge, no geographic
overlap, deepest cultural distance from your background. LNG (fit 4) is fascinating and
lucrative but the same entry problem plus mandatory relocation. **Power trading (route 9,
fit 6) is the defensible member of this family** — GB power is where your knowledge actually
counts, and HC Group flags "short-term power" and "analytics-enabled trading" as premium
areas [FACT]. If trading appeals, go at power, via analytics, and do not go at oil.

## 4. Part 6 — My recommendation

You asked for an evidence-based recommendation before being asked to choose. Here it is.

### 1. Strongest primary route — **Infrastructure investment, entered via technical advisory**

Formally: **LTA/TDD specialisation → infrastructure debt or renewable infrastructure PE.**

*Reasoning.* It is the only route where the first two years require no job change, no
relocation, no pay cut and no credential — you are simply asking to be staffed on work your
employer already sells. It converts your existing expertise into transaction currency rather
than discarding it. It has an acceptable median (£150–220k) *and* a real tail (carry). Its
failure mode is benign: you become a senior technical adviser, which is a good outcome. And
it works from Edinburgh, with London and New York both open later.

### 2. Strongest secondary route — **Data-centre energy strategy**

*Reasoning.* The steepest demand growth in the sector [FACT: IEA, PJM], the fewest people
who can do all of engineering + power markets + procurement, and a possible New Jersey
period that would place you inside PJM. Pursue this *in parallel* — the knowledge overlaps
heavily with route 1 (Modules B, C, E, F, N), so it costs few extra hours until the point of
specialisation.

### 3. Strongest entrepreneurial route — **Productised technical-commercial tooling, then co-development**

*Reasoning.* Not a startup in the venture sense. You have GIS, energy modelling, Python and
TDD experience, and you have already built a battery dispatch model. The credible sequence
is: build tools that make your own diligence faster → license or sell them to developers and
funds → use the relationships to co-develop projects for a development fee plus retained
equity. **Retained development equity is the realistic route to multi-million net worth**,
far more so than salary. Do not quit to do this; build it alongside.

### 4. Lower-risk route — **Consultancy leadership with a transaction-advisory tilt** (route 14)

Explicitly your floor. Build the practice, become CEng, take the director track. £90–180k,
Scotland, stable. Everything else is a call option written on top of this.

### 5. Higher-risk, higher-upside — **Developer commercial with promote/retained equity** (route 3)

Real P&L, real equity participation, genuine multi-million potential if projects reach FID
and you hold a stake. Higher variance: development projects fail, and planning and grid
timelines are outside your control.

### 6. Attractive but probably a poor fit — **Oil and LNG trading** (routes 21, 22)

Highest headline compensation on the list. But: no transferable knowledge, no geographic
overlap with Scotland, lateral entry at 30 is rare, the market is currently employer-led with
moderated bonus pools [FACT: HC Group], and the median outcome is far below the tail everyone
quotes. **I am recommending against it, and I would want you to argue back if you disagree.**

### 7. Testable without leaving your job — **all of routes 1, 2, 3, 8 and 14 simultaneously**

This is the structural advantage of your position and the reason the programme is designed
the way it is. Asking to be staffed on LTA and TDD mandates tests routes 1 and 2. Building
Projects 1–3 tests routes 3 and 8. Nothing here requires resigning to find out whether you
like it. **Test first, commit second.**

## 5. The honest probability statement

You asked for honesty about the £200k+ / multi-million target.

**[ESTIMATE], and I want to be precise about what this is:** on a 10-year horizon, pursuing
the recommended primary and secondary routes seriously — meaning the programme is completed,
transaction exposure is obtained, and you move employer at least once —

- **P(total compensation > £200k at some point) ≈ 45–60%.** Achievable. Not assured.
- **P(sustained > £200k for 5+ consecutive years) ≈ 30–40%.**
- **P(net worth > £2m by ~2041, age ~45) ≈ 25–35%**, and this depends far more on
  **equity or carry participation and savings rate** than on salary. See `06-wealth-strategy.md`.
- **P(career stagnation — still doing essentially today's job at similar real pay in 2036)
  ≈ 15–25%**, concentrated in the scenario where you study but never obtain transaction
  exposure or change employer.

**The dominant risk is not failure. It is drift** — completing modules, accumulating
knowledge, and never converting it into a different seat. The roadmap is built with forcing
decision points specifically to counter that.
